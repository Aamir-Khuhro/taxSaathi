## Who this is for

You have received a notice from FBR, for income tax or sales tax, by email, on IRIS or on paper, and you are not sure what to do next.

## The short answer

Read the whole notice, find the date by which you must respond, and start collecting the records it asks about. Most notices ask for information or an explanation. A clear written reply, sent before the date, is the normal way to deal with one. Ignoring a notice is the one thing that reliably makes things worse.

## Hour 1: read it properly

- **Read every page, including attachments.** The question being asked is often on the second page.
- **Find the key details:** the tax year or period, the section of the law it is issued under, the date it was issued and the **compliance date**.
- **Check it is genuine.** Notices are issued through FBR's IRIS system, so check your IRIS account for the same notice. Be wary of calls or messages asking for payment or passwords.

## The first day: work out what is being asked

Notices usually fall into a few kinds:

- **A request for information or documents.** FBR wants records or an explanation.
- **A notice about a missing return or statement.** Something should have been filed and was not.
- **A show-cause notice.** FBR proposes an addition, penalty or demand and asks why it should not proceed.
- **A notice to amend an assessment, or an audit selection.** These are more involved and usually need careful preparation.

Knowing which kind you have tells you how much work the reply needs.

## The second day: collect the records

- The return, statement or filing the notice is about
- Bank statements and tax deducted certificates for that period
- Any earlier letters or replies on the same matter
- Anything that supports your position

## What not to do

- **Do not ignore it** because the amount looks small or the notice looks routine.
- **Do not reply in a hurry** with an explanation you cannot support with documents.
- **Do not share your IRIS password or OTPs** with anyone who contacts you about the notice.
- **Do not wait until the last day.** If you genuinely need more time, a written request before the compliance date is the proper route. Whether more time is allowed is the officer's decision, so do not rely on it.

## Deadlines

The compliance date on the notice is the deadline that matters. Note it somewhere you will see it. If it is within seven days, treat the matter as urgent.
